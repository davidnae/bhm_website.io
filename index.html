Adelphi Black History Map

Black History Month UK / October 2026 / Honouring Our Communities

Twenty MinutesFrom The Adelphi

Black-owned businesses and Black British history, measured in walking minutes from 1–11 John Adam Street. Every entry has a named owner and was confirmed trading in September 2026.

Why this, this year The UK theme for 2026 is Honouring Our Communities — the everyday work that sustains Black life in Britain, rather than only its landmark names. Spending money seven minutes from your desk is a more literal reading of that than a portrait wall.

The map

Rings are walking minutes · bearings are true

Lunch break

Under fifteen minutes. You can be back at your desk.

1

38 King Street

Heritage · plaque only

The Africa Centre’s home from 1964 to 2013. Desmond Tutu met Thabo Mbeki at the bar; from 1985 Jazzie B ran the Soul II Soul sound system here, and the Limpopo Club put on Youssou N’Dour and Angélique Kidjo. It is retail now, which is its own piece of the story.

38 King Street, Covent Garden WC2E 8JS
Note: the charity’s later Southwark building closed in July 2026, so King Street is the site to mark.

5min walk
2

National Portrait Gallery

Heritage · free entry

Holds the only known painted portrait of Mary Seacole — lost for more than a century, identified in 2003, hanging here now. Free, and open at lunchtime.

St Martin’s Place WC2H 0HE

7min walk
3

Jamaica Patty Co

Jamaican patties & bakery

Started in 2013 because you could not get a decent patty in London. Cheap, quick, and the realistic option for a team lunch run.

26 New Row WC2N 4LA
Theresa Roberts, Jamaican-born art curator, with chef Collin Brown

7min walk
4

Enish

Nigerian restaurant & Afrobeats lounge

Jollof, suya and grills, with live music later on. The name is a portmanteau of the founders’ own — Eniola and Shola — which tells you what kind of business it is.

5 Great Newport Street WC2H 7JB
Olushola & Eniola Medupin

8min walk
5

Ikoyi

West African fine dining · two Michelin stars

The only Black-owned business on the Strand itself, and the least likely lunch: a £395 tasting menu built on West African spicing and British seasonality, named Best Global Restaurant at the 2026 Tastemakers Awards. A pin for pride rather than payday.

180 Strand WC2R 1EA
Iré Hassan-Odukale, co-founder, with chef Jeremy Chan

11min walk
6

Limin Southbank

Trinidadian kitchen & rum bar

Best Caribbean Restaurant at the UK Caribbean Food Awards, with a sand floor in summer. Over Waterloo Bridge, so it doubles as a walk. The obvious pick if you want one booking for the whole office.

Gabriel’s Wharf, 56 Upper Ground SE1 9PP
Sham Mahabir, Trinidadian chef, co-owner

13min walk
7

GABU Chocolate

Bean-to-bar chocolatier

Chocolate made by hand on the premises from single-origin cocoa from Grenada, the Dominican Republic and Madagascar. Same courtyard as Limin — one trip, two entries, and something to hand round the office.

Unit 7, Gabriel’s Wharf, 56 Upper Ground SE1 9PP
Lisette Davis

13min walk
8

Mandela, Parliament Square

Heritage · outdoors, free

Ian Walters’ bronze, unveiled in 2007 after a campaign to put it in the square with the prime ministers rather than somewhere quieter.

Parliament Square SW1P 3JX

16min walk

After work

Fifteen to thirty minutes on foot, or a few stops on the tube.

9

Mary Seacole statue

Heritage · outdoors, free

The first statue in Britain of a named Black woman, unveiled in 2016 by Baroness Floella Benjamin after a twelve-year campaign. Martin Jennings’ bronze stands in the hospital gardens facing Parliament, which is not an accident.

St Thomas’ Hospital gardens, Lambeth Palace Road SE1 7EH

18min walk

Sista Barista

Independent café

A corner café run by two sisters with their daughters — speciality coffee, homemade breakfasts, 4.8 stars across several hundred reviews. Exactly the kind of business this year’s theme is pointing at.

141 Westminster Bridge Road SE1 7HR
Wendy & Kay, with their daughters

21min walk

Akoko

Live-fire cooking and West African spicing, starred again in the 2026 Michelin Guide.

Chishuru

Modern West African · one Michelin star

Adejoké Bakare took a star in 2024, reportedly the first Black woman anywhere to hold one for her own restaurant. She began with a Brixton pop-up won through a competition for the site.

3 Great Titchfield Street W1W 8AX
Adejoké Bakare, chef-founder

13

Olaudah Equiano’s house

Heritage · plaque only

He lodged here in 1789, the year he published The Interesting Narrative — the book that made the abolitionist case in the voice of a man who had been enslaved, and became a bestseller doing it.

73 Riding House Street W1W 7EJ
Green plaque by Westminster City Council

29min walk

Connaught Village

Café & art gallery

Two stops on one street. Saint Aymes at 59 Connaught Street is the café and chocolatier run by sisters Lois and Michela Wilson. CasildART at number 32 is Sukai Eccleston’s gallery for contemporary Black art, which also runs an annual Black and Global Majority art fair.

Connaught Street, W2
Lois & Michela Wilson · Sukai Eccleston

17

Dark Sugars — Brick Lane

Bean-to-bar chocolatier

Founded in 1997 by a Ghanaian-born chocolatier who researched cocoa on her own family’s farm, trading from a Borough Market stall before opening on Brick Lane in 2013. Two more shops in Greenwich.

141 Brick Lane E1 6SB
also 9 & 21 Nelson Road, Greenwich SE10 9JB

25by tube
18

Brixton

Archive & bookshop · archive free

The Black Cultural Archives on Windrush Square is the only national heritage centre for Black British history: archives, museum objects and a reference library, free to enter. Thursday to Saturday 10–6 and Sunday 12–5, closed Monday to Wednesday, reading room by appointment. Five minutes away, Round Table Books in Brixton Village is Aimée Felone’s shop for Black and Global Majority authors — the place to buy the office book display.

1 Windrush Square SW2 1EF
73 Granville Arcade, Brixton Village SW9 8PS
Heads up: Round Table Books is crowdfunding a move, so check before you go.

25by tube
19

New Beacon Books

Bookshop & publisher

Britain’s first Black publisher and specialist bookshop, founded in 1966 by John La Rose and Sarah White and now run by his son Michael. It nearly closed in 2021 and was saved by a £76,000 crowdfunder. It turned sixty this year, which is the best single fact on this map.

76 Stroud Green Road N4 3EN
Michael La Rose · Tue–Sat 11–6, Thu to 8

30by tube

Notes before you print it

What was checked

Every business here has a named owner and a source, and was confirmed trading in 2026. Entries that appear on “Black-owned London” roundups with no Black owner findable anywhere were dropped — including one Covent Garden café on several such lists whose name turns out to refer to a postage stamp.

Where a business has one Black founder alongside others, this says so rather than smoothing it over: Ikoyi, Limin and Chishuru are all co-founded.

Worth a phone call first

  • Chishuru’s postcode — sources split between W1W 8AX and 8AU.
  • Sweet Dee’s Selfridges counter — their own site has not been updated since 2024.
  • Dark Sugars’ founder — published sources give her name three different ways, so the copy here leaves it out rather than print it wrong.
  • Round Table Books — actively raising money to move premises.

The honest gap

Covent Garden and the Strand are thin. Ikoyi is the only Black-owned business on the Strand itself and the nearest real cluster is over the river. Better to say that on the display than to pad the map: the West End is expensive, and whose businesses survive here is part of the history.

Three places that would have been on this map closed this year — the Africa Centre’s Southwark building, and Little Baobab and Tatale inside it.

Making it a display

  • A1 for a main wall, A3 by the kitchen or the lifts.
  • Pin a blank sheet beside it headed “Been? Add one”. The map gets better, and it stops being something people walk past.
  • Book one team lunch — Limin takes groups, Jamaica Patty Co is the cheap round.
  • Buy the book display from Round Table Books or New Beacon, not Amazon.
